Business model · concept stage

A safety company that happens to sell a copilot.

Wayline's model: hardware priced near cost, an open-source core that compounds through its community, safety features that are free forever — and recurring revenue from the services people actually value: connectivity, a human response center, and fleet tools.

The problem

The hardware is already in the car. The safety net isn't.

Our mission is to make every car on the road a little smarter and a lot less lonely in an emergency — without asking anyone to buy a new car.

Great sensors, timid software

Millions of cars ship with the cameras, radar and steering actuators needed for lane-keep and adaptive cruise — but the factory software often uses only a fraction of what that hardware can do.

When nobody can make the call

After a serious crash or a medical event at the wheel, the person who most needs help is often the one least able to ask for it — especially when driving alone.

Safety services are tied to new cars

Connected-safety features are usually bundled with new vehicles and brand subscriptions. The cars people already own — and keep for years — are left out.

The flywheel

Open source is the growth engine.

Every driver who installs Wayline can — if they choose — contribute anonymized driving data, and every developer can contribute support for a new car. Both make the product better for the next driver.

  • Lower acquisition cost: community, word of mouth and creators instead of expensive ad spend
  • Faster coverage: community car ports extend the list of supported models
  • Trust: anyone can audit the code that steers their car
  • Privacy by design: data sharing is opt-in, and drivers can delete their data

Go-to-market

Win the builders first, then the families, then the fleets.

Phase A

Developers & enthusiasts

Open-source community, simulator and dev kits. They find bugs, port cars and become the first evangelists.

Phase B

Safety-minded drivers

Long commuters, people who often drive alone, and families of new or older drivers — sold on Guardian as much as on lane centering.

Phase C

Fleets

Delivery, field-service and rideshare operators, where faster incident response and coaching have a clear return.

Phase D

Insurers & partners

Opt-in insurance programs and integration partnerships that lower the effective cost of Wayline for drivers.

Risks & mitigations

What could go wrong — and what we do about it.

Driver-assistance regulation

Rules for aftermarket driver assistance differ between countries and can change.

MitigationLaunch market by market with conservative limits, mandatory driver monitoring for engagement, and compliance review before each release.

Safety & liability

A driver-assistance product can be misused or can make mistakes.

MitigationSupervised Level 2 only, independent torque and rate limits, driver override always wins, extensive simulation and closed-course testing, and appropriate insurance.

Emergency-call rules

Automated calls to emergency numbers are restricted in many places.

MitigationNo automated calls to emergency numbers. Core alerts personal contacts; Plus routes through a trained 24/7 response center that requests emergency services.

Car makers lock down interfaces

New vehicles may restrict the interfaces Wayline relies on.

MitigationBroad multi-brand coverage, community car ports, and partnerships with fleets and suppliers.

AI reliability

Language models can be slow, unavailable or wrong.

MitigationAI never gates escalation. A deterministic on-device timer decides; offline templates replace AI briefs when needed; distress words always override a cancel.

Privacy & trust

Cameras in a car are sensitive.

MitigationOn-device processing, opt-in uploads and data sharing, deletion controls, and code anyone can audit.
